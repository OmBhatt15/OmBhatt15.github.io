As a member of the Monash Unmanned Aerial Systems electronics team, I am responsible for the Measurement & Logging Board for Project Cassowary, a high-fidelity thrust test stand used to characterise drone propulsion systems.

PCB design and hardware architecture

Role

I led the hardware development for the Measurement & Logging hub: presiding over design reviews with industry representatives, selecting high-precision components (Texas Instruments ADCs and instrumentation amplifiers), and routing a 4-layer PCB in Altium Designer that keeps sensitive analog signals away from high-current motor noise.

Technical challenges

Capturing thrust and torque at 50 kS/s needs a 0.1% precision analog front end. I used the INA826 instrumentation amplifier to boost millivolt load-cell signals while rejecting interference from 120 A motor surges, and a dual-rail LMR14050 buck converter system for independent 3.3 V and 5 V rails so the RP2040 logic stays protected from back-EMF spikes.

Project ECKO

ARES Technologies. Hardware and PCB design. 2026 – present

In active development
Assistive technology4-layer PCBAltium DesignerPower integrityEmbedded systems

A head-worn assistive device that converts distance into touch. Time-of-flight sensors measure the distance to surrounding objects, and an array of 32 haptic motors renders those measurements as vibration, so a visually impaired wearer can perceive obstacles and room layout. I am responsible for the 4-layer control board.

Board bring-up is in progress. The schematic is complete and routed in Altium Designer; photographs and test data will be published once the first boards return from fabrication.

The challenge

A cane or guide dog mostly detects obstacles at ground level and within arm's reach, leaving head-height hazards and the broader layout of a space hard to perceive. Rendering that through touch needs a high actuator count in a compact, USB-powered, head-worn assembly.

The time-of-flight sensors ship with a single fixed I²C address, so they cannot share a bus directly. And 32 motors switching together generate transients that can disrupt the 2.4 GHz radio and corrupt the sensor bus.

Engineering solution

An I²C multiplexer driven by a reset line from the ESP32-S3 selects each sensor onto the bus in turn. Motor drive uses two PCA9685 16-channel PWM controllers feeding four ULN2803C Darlington arrays.

To contain switching noise I specified a 4-layer stack-up with a solid ground plane on L2 and a split 3V3 / +5V_MOT plane on L3, so every power and ground pin reaches its plane through a via rather than a trace. The logic rail is fed through a ferrite bead and a dedicated regulator, with an antenna keep-out across all four copper layers.

The challenge

When all five fingers actuated at once the power surge browned out standard microcontrollers. Mapping linear servo travel onto the non-linear rotation of finger joints also needed careful linkage design.

The challenge

Coordinating 18 servos, three per leg, into a smooth tripod gait. Breadboard wiring was too bulky and unreliable for a moving chassis and kept failing mid-walk.

Engineering solution

I wrote a custom inverse kinematics solver in Python that computes every joint angle from a desired foot coordinate (x, y, z). To fix the wiring, we moved the servo drivers and power management onto a custom KiCad PCB integrated into the chassis, cutting weight by 20%.

Project SENTINEL

Co-founder, ARES Technologies. Vision pipeline and control. 2025

Operational

An automated defence simulation using computer vision: it tracks aerial targets and aligns a turret in real time without human input. Exhibited at the Monash DeepNeuron Tech Expo.

The challenge

Processing video fast enough to track a moving object while also driving stepper motors. Latency between detection and motion made tracking lag behind the target.

Engineering solution

A multi-threaded Python architecture: one thread for the heavy OpenCV processing, a separate lightweight thread for motor control. The turret predicts the target's path by vector analysis instead of reacting to its last position, which noticeably improved accuracy.

x72 16-bit processor on FPGA

ECE2072 Digital Systems (Monash University). Team of 2. 2026

Completed. All four tasks functional
VerilogFPGAQuartus PrimeHardware verification

A 16-bit multi-cycle processor written in Verilog and implemented on a Terasic DE10-Lite (Intel MAX 10) board. It runs an eight-instruction set over a shared 16-bit bus and, in its final form, executes stored programs from on-chip ROM with conditional branching. Our demo program computes 5! with a loop of multiply, add and branch instructions and shows the result on the seven-segment displays.

The challenge

Every instruction shares one bus, so the control unit has to sequence the registers, ALU and bus multiplexer correctly across four clock ticks. One wrong enable signal corrupts a result silently, which makes verification as important as the design.

Engineering solution

I built the control unit, a one-hot tick FSM that needs no decoding, a width-parameterised register reused for every register in the design, the sign extender and the board top level. Self-checking testbenches compute each expected result independently: 40,323 checks over 2,267 instructions passed with no failures, and the design was then confirmed on hardware.

Real-time audio acquisition system

ECE2071 Systems Programming (Monash University). Team of 4. 2026

Completed. 39.75 / 40 in demonstration
Embedded CSTM32DMA / SPI / UARTSignal processing

A two-microcontroller audio recorder. One STM32L432 samples an analogue audio input at about 44.1 kS/s and passes the samples over SPI to a second STM32, which streams them over UART to a PC command-line tool. The tool records manually or triggers from an ultrasonic distance sensor, and saves each recording as a WAV file with CSV data, waveform, FFT and spectrogram plots.

The challenge

Holding a CD-quality sample rate end to end leaves little headroom: the UART link to the PC is the bottleneck, and a single corrupted packet audibly damages the recording.

Engineering solution

Data moves by DMA on both microcontrollers so the CPU is free between buffers. Two 12-bit samples are packed into three bytes to cut UART bandwidth, and every packet carries a CRC-8 that the PC checks so corrupted packets are dropped rather than written to the audio. A moving-average filter with outlier rejection cleans the signal. Code was managed in Git with feature branches and reproducible Python environments.

Regulated 3.3 V power supply tender

ECE2131 Electrical Circuits (Monash University). Team of 5. 2026

Completed. Tender scored 94.7%
Analog circuit designLTspiceSoldered prototypeCost analysis

A tender response for a regulated 3.3 V supply to power an STM32L432-based embedded audio system. The design was analysed, simulated in LTspice, built as a soldered prototype and costed for mass production. Markers singled out the report and the layout of the soldered prototype.

The challenge

Hold a stable output under both steady and switching loads, survive a reversed or excessive input, and come in under $5 AUD per unit at volume using parts available from more than one supplier.

Engineering solution

An LM317 regulator with a precision resistor divider and input, output and adjustment capacitors to suppress ripple. A clamping diode gives reverse-polarity protection, and the regulator's headroom plus a Zener clamp handle overvoltage. Tested at DC and with dynamic loads at 1 MHz and 80 MHz.

Image aesthetics prediction

ECE2191 Probability & AI for Engineers (Monash University). 2026

Completed
PythonMachine learningscikit-learnTransfer learning

A model that predicts how people rate the aesthetic quality of photographs, as the final task of an assignment that also covered robust (Huber) regression, gradient descent with momentum and outlier detection.

The challenge

Aesthetic judgements depend on composition, colour and texture more than on what an object is, so the final-layer features of an image-classification network are a weak fit.

Engineering solution

A frozen, pretrained ResNet-50 with features pooled from all four of its stages rather than just the last, feeding an RBF kernel ridge regressor tuned by 5-fold cross-validation. Expected error fell to an MSE of about 0.30, against 0.50 for predicting the mean and 0.40 for the standard last-layer approach.

Self-play card game AI agent

AI agent developer, Pokémon TCG AI Battle Challenge (Kaggle). 2026

Completed
PythonMonte Carlo tree searchGenetic algorithmsSelf-play RL

An agent that autonomously constructs and refines a competitive deck from a pool of more than 18,000 cards, trained through over 250,000 simulated self-play battles to produce a high-win-rate, meta-resistant deck.

The challenge

A static heuristic evaluates one option at a time and plays greedily. It cannot value a move whose payoff only arrives later in the turn, so it loses to multi-step combinations. Both the deck and the play policy also have to evolve against a shifting meta.

Engineering solution

Monte Carlo tree search with UCB1 selection evaluates candidate turn actions by rollout, combined with a beam search over multi-step sequences that keeps the top-K partial plans. A genetic algorithm evolves about 20 weighted terms of the board-evaluation heuristic, using self-play tournaments as the fitness function so the opponent improves alongside the agent.
