As a member of the Monash Unmanned Aerial Systems (MUAS) electronics team, I am responsible for the design and development of the Measurement & Logging Board (Board 1) for Project Cassowary — a high-fidelity thrust test stand used to characterise drone propulsion systems.

PCB Design & Hardware Architecture

Role & Responsibilities

I led the hardware development for the Measurement & Logging hub. My work involved presiding over design reviews with industry representatives, selecting high-precision components (Texas Instruments ADCs and instrumentation amplifiers), and routing a 4-layer PCB in Altium Designer focused on isolating sensitive analog signals from high-current motor noise.

Technical Challenges

Signal Integrity: Capturing thrust and torque data at 50 kS/s requires a 0.1% precision analog front-end. I utilised the INA826 instrumentation amplifier to boost millivolt-scale load cell signals while rejecting electromagnetic interference from 120 A motor surges.

Power Domain Isolation: I designed a dual-rail buck converter system (LMR14050) to provide independent 3.3 V and 5 V power, ensuring the RP2040 logic remains protected from back-EMF spikes.

During our V3 design review, we rigorously evaluated the system's power architecture. This ensured a greater than 2x safety margin while avoiding unnecessary over-engineering and keeping board fabrication costs down.

Project ECKO

ARES Technologies — Hardware & PCB design · 2026 – Present

In Active Development
Assistive Technology 4-Layer PCB Design Altium Designer Power Integrity Embedded Systems

A head-worn assistive device that converts spatial distance into haptic feedback. Time-of-flight sensors measure the distance to surrounding objects, and an array of 32 haptic motors renders those measurements as vibration, allowing a visually impaired wearer to perceive obstacles and room layout. I am responsible for the design of the 4-layer control board.

Hardware
Board bring-up is in progress. The schematic is complete and routed in Altium Designer; photographs and test data will be published once the first boards return from fabrication.

The Challenge

A cane or guide dog primarily detects obstacles at ground level and within arm's reach, leaving hazards at head height and the broader layout of a space difficult to perceive. Rendering that information through touch requires a high actuator count within a compact, head-worn, USB-powered assembly.

This creates two electrical constraints. The time-of-flight sensors ship with a single fixed I²C address and therefore cannot share a bus directly. Separately, 32 vibration motors switching simultaneously generate transients capable of disrupting the 2.4 GHz radio and corrupting the sensor bus.

Engineering Solution

I resolved the addressing constraint with an I²C multiplexer driven by a reset line from the ESP32-S3, selecting each sensor onto the bus in sequence. Motor drive is handled by two PCA9685 16-channel PWM controllers feeding four ULN2803C Darlington arrays.

To contain switching noise, I specified a 4-layer stack-up with a solid ground plane on L2 and a split 3V3 / +5V_MOT plane on L3, so every power and ground pin reaches its plane through a via rather than a trace. The logic rail is fed through a ferrite bead and a dedicated regulator, and a keep-out region beneath the antenna is applied across all four copper layers.

Project JAEGAR

Co-Founder, ARES Technologies — Mechanical design & power electronics · 2025 – 2026

Operational
Bio-Mimetic Robotics High-Current Power Distribution SolidWorks CAD

An anthropomorphic robotic arm designed to replicate human motion in real time. The primary goal was to create a fluid, high-torque system capable of mimicking delicate hand gestures for medical and teleoperation applications. The end goal is a full-scale humanoid robot that can mimic a user's actions.

Phase 1: Finger Actuation Iterations
Phase 2: Full Hand Assembly

The Challenge

The main difficulty was handling the power surge when all five fingers actuated simultaneously. Standard microcontrollers could not supply the required current draw, leading to brownouts. Additionally, mapping linear servo movement to the non-linear rotation of human finger joints required complex mechanical linkage design.

Engineering Solution

I designed a dedicated power distribution bus separate from the logic controller to isolate noise and manage high loads. For the mechanics, I used SolidWorks to iterate on a tendon-driven design, routing high-tension nylon line through 3D-printed phalanges to simulate muscle contraction. The finger mechanism progressed through four design generations before reaching its current form.

A hexapod robot built to assist with daily tasks, surveillance and exploration. This platform served as a testbed for inverse kinematics algorithms and custom PCB fabrication.

Design Evolution: Leg Mechanisms
Chassis & Integration
Custom Electronics & PCB Design

The Challenge

Coordinating 18 separate servos (three per leg) to achieve a smooth tripod gait walking cycle. Traditional breadboard wiring was too bulky and unreliable for a moving chassis, causing frequent connection failures during movement.

Engineering Solution

I developed a custom inverse kinematics solver in Python to calculate the precise angle of every joint based on a desired foot coordinate (x, y, z). To resolve the wiring issue, I designed a custom PCB in KiCad that integrated the servo drivers and power management directly into the robot's chassis, reducing weight by 20%.

Project SENTINEL

Co-Founder, ARES Technologies — Vision pipeline & control · 2025

Operational
Computer Vision Python / OpenCV Real-Time Tracking

An automated defence simulation system using computer vision. Designed to track aerial targets and align a turret mechanism in real time without human input.

The Challenge

Processing video feeds fast enough to track a moving object while simultaneously driving stepper motors. Latency between the camera detecting an object and the motors moving created a lag that made the tracking inaccurate.

Engineering Solution

I implemented a multi-threaded Python script in which one thread handled the heavy OpenCV image processing and a separate lightweight thread handled motor control. This allowed the turret to predict the target's path through vector analysis rather than reacting to its previous position, significantly improving accuracy.

Self-Play Card Game AI Agent

AI Agent Developer, Pokémon TCG AI Battle Challenge (Kaggle) · 2026

Completed
Python Monte Carlo Tree Search Genetic Algorithms Self-Play Reinforcement Learning

An agent that autonomously constructs and refines a competitive deck from a pool of more than 18,000 cards, trained through over 250,000 simulated self-play battles to produce a high-win-rate, meta-resistant deck.

The Challenge

A static heuristic evaluates one option at a time and therefore plays greedily. It cannot assign value to a move whose payoff only materialises later in the turn, which causes it to lose consistently against multi-step combinations.

The problem is compounded by the fact that both the deck composition and the play policy must evolve against a shifting competitive meta.

Engineering Solution

I replaced static scoring with Monte Carlo Tree Search using UCB1 selection to evaluate candidate turn actions by rollout, combined with a beam search across multi-step action sequences that retains the top-K partial plans at each step.

A genetic algorithm evolves approximately 20 weighted terms of the board-evaluation heuristic, using iterated self-play tournaments as the fitness function so that the agent's opponent improves alongside it.
