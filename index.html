Measurement & Logging Board

Electronics Team Member · Monash Unmanned Aerial Systems · Hardware lead, Board 1

A 4-layer mixed-signal board that captures thrust, torque and power from a heavy-lift drone propulsion test stand at 50 kS/s — while sitting inches from 120 A of switching motor current.

50 kS/sacquisition rate
0.1%front-end precision
120 Amotor EMI rejected
4-layerAltium Designer
PCB design & hardware architecture

Problem

Manufacturer motor and propeller datasheets can't be taken on trust, so Project Cassowary needed a test stand that measures real thrust, torque and power before any propulsion hardware is signed off for flight.

The measurement problem is the hard part: load cells output millivolts, and they do it right next to a motor drawing surges of up to 120 A. Every bit of that switching noise couples into the signal you are trying to resolve to 0.1%.

Approach

  • Owned hardware development of the Measurement & Logging hub (Board 1) and presented it through formal design reviews with industry representatives.
  • Selected a high-precision analog front-end around the INA826 instrumentation amplifier to lift millivolt-scale load-cell output while rejecting common-mode EMI.
  • Routed a 4-layer board in Altium Designer with the analog section partitioned away from high-current return paths.
  • Designed a dual-rail LMR14050 buck converter stage giving independent 3.3 V and 5 V domains, isolating the RP2040 logic from back-EMF transients.

Engineering decisions

Resolving a millivolt signal next to a 120 A motor
Symptom
Load-cell readings that track motor RPM rather than actual thrust.
Root cause
Bridge output is millivolt-scale; ESC switching and motor current surges couple straight into it.
Fix
INA826 instrumentation amplifier with high common-mode rejection, placed close to the sensor, with analog and high-current sections separated in the layout.
Sizing the buck converters without over-engineering the board
Context
At the V3 design review the whole power architecture was re-examined.
Analysis
Confirmed propulsion testing runs from a 4S LiPo — 16.8 V peak — so I validated the 40 V-rated LMR14050 against that worst case.
Result
Better than 2× voltage safety margin while avoiding unnecessary over-specification, keeping fabrication cost down.

Haptic Sight Blindfold

ARES Technologies · Hardware & PCB design

A head-worn assistive device that turns distance into touch: time-of-flight sensors measure the space around the wearer, and 32 haptic motors render it as vibration they can feel.

32haptic motor channels
4-layersplit-plane PCB
ESP32-S3WROOM-1 module
ToFdistance sensing
Hardware
Board bring-up in progress Schematic complete and routed in Altium; photos and test data go here once the first boards return from fab.

Problem

A white cane or a guide dog mostly senses what is at ground level and within arm's reach. Obstacles at head height, and the broader layout of a room, stay invisible.

Rendering that information as touch needs a lot of actuators in a small, head-worn, USB-powered package — and 32 vibration motors switching at once make for a hostile electrical environment around a 2.4 GHz radio and an I²C sensor bus.

Approach

  • Designed the main board around an ESP32-S3-WROOM-1, with a keep-out region under the antenna applied across all four copper layers.
  • Fanned the time-of-flight sensors out through an I²C multiplexer, since the sensors ship with one fixed address and cannot otherwise share a bus.
  • Drove 32 motor channels from two PCA9685 16-channel PWM controllers into four ULN2803C Darlington arrays.
  • Built a 4-layer stack-up — L1 signal, L2 solid ground, L3 split 3V3 / +5V_MOT, L4 signal — so every power and ground pin reaches its plane through a via rather than a trace.
  • Separated the motor rail from the logic rail with a ferrite bead and a dedicated LDO, and protected the USB-C input with an ESD array, a fuse, a reverse-polarity diode and 470 µF of bulk capacitance.

Challenges

Several ToF sensors, one hard-wired I²C address
Symptom
Only one sensor can be addressed if they all sit on the same bus.
Root cause
The sensors expose a single fixed I²C address with no strapping option.
Fix
An I²C multiplexer with a reset line driven from the ESP32, so each sensor is selected onto the bus in turn.
32 motors sharing a supply with the logic and the radio
Symptom
Motor switching transients threaten brownouts and corrupt the I²C bus.
Root cause
A single 5 V rail feeding both the Darlington arrays and the regulator couples motor current into the logic domain.
Fix
Split the L3 plane into separate 3V3 and +5V_MOT regions with a clear gap, fed the logic side through a ferrite bead, and kept a strip either side of the split free of vias.
Routing USB-C differential pairs out of a crowded connector
Symptom
D− has a pad on either side of D+, and the CC pull-down is boxed in by the data pads.
Root cause
USB-C pin symmetry puts both halves of each pair on opposite sides of the connector footprint.
Fix
Dropped the trapped pins through vias under the connector body to rejoin on the bottom layer, and moved I²C_SDA to a free GPIO so it never has to cross the USB pair.

Bio-Mimetic Robotic Hand

Co-Founder, ARES Technologies · Mechanical design & power electronics

A tendon-driven anthropomorphic hand that mirrors a human operator's motion in real time, built toward a full-scale humanoid that can copy a user's actions for medical and teleoperation work.

5independently actuated fingers
4finger design iterations
Real-timemotion mimicry
Custompower distribution bus
Latest demonstration
Phase 1 — finger actuation iterations V1 → V4
Phase 2 — full hand assembly

Problem

Replicating a human hand needs many degrees of freedom packed into a hand-sized volume, and the joints don't move linearly — a servo's rotation has to be mapped onto the non-linear rotation of a human finger joint through mechanical linkage.

Electrically, actuating all five fingers at once draws a current surge that a standard microcontroller supply simply cannot deliver, which showed up as brownouts mid-grip.

Approach

  • Iterated the finger mechanism through four generations in SolidWorks, tuning linkage geometry to match anatomical range of motion.
  • Built a tendon-driven design: high-tension nylon line routed through 3D-printed phalanges to emulate muscle contraction.
  • Designed a dedicated high-current power distribution bus, separate from the logic controller, to carry peak servo load and keep switching noise off the control lines.
  • Mapped operator glove input to servo targets for real-time mimicry.

Challenges

Brownouts when all five fingers actuated together
Symptom
Servos stuttered and the controller reset during full-hand grips.
Root cause
Combined stall current from simultaneous actuation exceeded what the original supply and wiring could deliver.
Fix
A dedicated power distribution bus sized for peak current, electrically separated from the logic controller.
Result
Full-hand actuation without supply collapse.
Linear servo travel vs. non-linear finger rotation
Symptom
Fingers curled unevenly and didn't reach a natural closed pose.
Root cause
A servo sweeps a constant angle per unit of travel; a finger joint does not.
Fix
Four rounds of linkage geometry in SolidWorks, converging on a tendon routing that reproduces anatomical motion.

Autonomous Hexapod

Co-Founder, ARES Technologies · Inverse kinematics & PCB design

A six-legged robot for surveillance and exploration, walking on a tripod gait driven by a custom inverse-kinematics solver, with all 18 servos and power management folded onto one board inside the chassis.

18coordinated servos
6 × 3DOF per leg
−20%chassis weight after integration
3leg mechanism revisions
Assembled platform
Design evolution — leg mechanisms V1 → V3
Chassis & integration
Custom electronics

Problem

A hexapod has to place each foot precisely while eighteen servos — three per leg — move in coordination. Commanding them by angle is unworkable; the useful input is a foot coordinate, which means solving the kinematics in reverse, every cycle.

The first prototype was breadboard-wired, and a nest of jumper connections on a walking, flexing chassis failed constantly.

Approach

  • Wrote a custom inverse-kinematics solver in Python that converts a desired foot coordinate (x, y, z) into the precise angle for every joint in that leg.
  • Modelled chassis and leg linkages in SolidWorks, iterating the leg mechanism through three revisions before committing to print.
  • Designed a custom PCB in KiCad integrating the servo drivers and power management directly into the chassis.
  • Added a camera and OpenCV object recognition tied to a gripper for autonomous pick-up.

Challenges

Breadboard wiring couldn't survive a walking robot
Symptom
Frequent connection failures and servo dropouts whenever the chassis moved.
Root cause
Dozens of jumpers carrying both power and signal worked loose under vibration, and the harness was too bulky to mount cleanly.
Fix
Moved servo drivers and power management onto one custom KiCad PCB integrated into the chassis.
Result
Chassis weight cut by 20% and the loose-connection failure mode eliminated.
Coordinating a tripod gait across 18 joints
Symptom
Gait transitions looked unstable and foot placement drifted.
Root cause
Per-joint angle commands carry no notion of where the foot actually needs to land.
Fix
Drove every leg from the IK solver instead, so the gait is planned in foot-coordinate space and the joint angles fall out of it.

PRJ-01 / SENTINEL Operational 2025

Computer-Vision Tracking Turret

Co-Founder, ARES Technologies · Vision pipeline & control

An aerial-defence simulation that acquires a moving target with OpenCV and drives a turret to keep it centred — predicting where the target is going rather than chasing where it was.

Real-timetarget tracking
Multi-threadedvision / motor split
Vectorpath prediction
MK4current revision
Prototype & demonstration

Problem

Keeping a moving target centred means processing video fast enough to locate it while simultaneously driving motors to follow it. Those two jobs compete: heavy image processing stalls the motor loop.

The result was latency between detection and movement — a visible lag that made the turret consistently point at where the target had just been.

Approach

  • Built an OpenCV pipeline to segment and locate the target in each frame.
  • Split the system across threads in Python: one carrying the heavy image processing, a separate lightweight thread handling motor control.
  • Used the target's frame-to-frame motion to predict its path by vector analysis, so corrections anticipate rather than react.
  • Closed the loop from pixel offset to stepper motor commands for turret alignment.

Challenges

Tracking lag between detection and motion
Symptom
The turret trailed behind the target, degrading accuracy at speed.
Root cause
A single execution thread meant every frame of OpenCV processing blocked the motor control path.
Fix
Multi-threaded architecture separating vision from actuation, plus vector-based path prediction instead of pure reaction.
Result
Significantly improved tracking accuracy on moving targets.

Problem

Approach

Self-Play Card Game AI Agent

AI Agent Developer · Pokémon TCG AI Battle Challenge

An agent that builds its own competitive deck from a pool of more than 18,000 cards and improves it through over 250,000 simulated self-play battles.

18k+card pool
250k+self-play battles
~20evolved heuristic weights
Agent architecture
Deck builder → MCTS planner → GA heuristic tuner → self-play loop Architecture diagram and training-curve plots to be added.

Problem

A static heuristic plays greedily: it scores one option at a time, so it cannot value a move whose payoff only arrives later in the turn, and it loses to multi-step combinations.

Both halves of the problem move at once — the deck and the play policy each have to evolve against a shifting meta.

Approach

  • Monte Carlo Tree Search with UCB1 selection to evaluate candidate turn actions by rollout.
  • Beam search over multi-step action sequences, keeping the top-K partial plans at each step.
  • A genetic algorithm that evolves roughly 20 weighted terms of the board-evaluation heuristic.
  • Iterated self-play tournaments as the fitness function, so the agent's opponent improves alongside it.

Challenges

Greedy scoring missed combination turns
Symptom
Locally optimal single moves, consistently beaten by multi-step setups.
Root cause
Scoring actions one at a time cannot represent a play whose value is only realised two or three steps later.
Fix
Beam search across action sequences, retaining the top-K partial plans rather than committing to the best immediate move.
Result
Compositional plays that greedy scoring never found.
